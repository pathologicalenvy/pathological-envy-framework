Chapter Twenty

# Conditional Prediction After Reorganization

Prediction within this framework does not concern destiny, identity, or permanence. It concerns conditional expectation. Once reorganization has occurred, future behavior becomes more legible - not because the system is fixed, but because stabilization constrains the range of viable responses under recurring conditions.

Prediction here is therefore structural rather than teleological. It does not assert what must occur. It specifies what is likely to recur given unchanged constraints.

### Prediction Without Determinism

The capacity to predict follows directly from constraint. When stabilization reduces the space of possible responses, repetition becomes economical. The system selects what works, not what is ideal. Over time, this selection yields patterned behavior.

This does not imply inevitability. Prediction remains conditional on the persistence of:
-	environmental affordances,
-	energetic cost structures,
-	and exposure tolerance.

When these change, prediction degrades.

### What Is Being Predicted

The object of prediction is not internal experience, motivation, or belief. It is regulatory behavior under pressure. The framework predicts how a system is likely to respond when exposed, challenged, or destabilized again.

Stabilization configurations differ in what they make cheap, costly, or inaccessible. These differences generate predictable patterns of response.

### Configuration-Specific Expectations

Narrative stabilization predicts:
-	rapid reassertion of interpretive certainty,
-	resistance to ambiguity,
-	defensive maintenance of coherence.

Avoidant stabilization predicts:
-	withdrawal in response to threat,
-	attenuation of engagement,
-	preference for low-salience environments.

Power-based stabilization predicts:
-	assertion of control when challenged,
-	intolerance of asymmetry loss,
-	escalation in the face of constraint.

Psychotic stabilization predicts:
-	rigidity under contradiction,
-	re-imposition of internally enforced coherence,
-	destabilization when imposed meaning fails.

Non-convergent architectures predict:
-	context-specific shifts,
-	compartmentalized response patterns,
-	vulnerability to cross-exposure.

These are not traits. They are cost-minimizing responses within stabilized systems.

### Prediction as a Function of Constraint

The strength of prediction depends on how tightly constrained the configuration is. Highly rigid stabilizations produce strong, narrow predictions. Flexible configurations produce weaker, broader ones.

Prediction therefore scales with rigidity, not pathology.

### Environmental Sensitivity

Because stabilizations are environmentally supported, prediction must account for context. A configuration that appears durable in one environment may destabilize rapidly in another.

Prediction degrades when:
-	power erodes,
-	narratives lose plausibility,
-	withdrawal becomes untenable,
-	or imposed coherence encounters sustained contradiction.

This sensitivity reinforces the non-finality of all configurations described.

### Prediction and Change

Prediction does not preclude change. It specifies the conditions under which change is costly. When constraints shift sufficiently, destabilization becomes likely, and reorganization may recur.

Prediction is thus compatible with intervention, not opposed to it. It clarifies what must change for different outcomes to become viable.

### Ethical Use of Prediction

Prediction within this framework is descriptive, not justificatory. It explains why certain responses recur. It does not excuse harm, assign blame, or reduce individuals to configurations.

Misuse arises only when conditional expectation is mistaken for moral certainty.

### Limits of Prediction

No framework can predict novel environments, unprecedented constraints, or singular events. The present account does not attempt to do so. Its predictions apply only within the bounded conditions it specifies.

Where those bounds are exceeded, the framework yields to uncertainty.

### From Prediction to Scope

With conditional prediction formalized, the unification offered here reaches its practical limit. What remains is to clarify what the framework does not claim, and where its applicability ends.

The final chapter addresses these boundaries explicitly.
