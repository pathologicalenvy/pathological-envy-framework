Chapter Twenty-Two

# Diagnostic Categories as Stabilization Artifacts

Diagnostic categories commonly referred to as “personality disorders” are not arbitrary. They recur with sufficient regularity to suggest structure. Yet they are also inconsistent, overlapping, and internally heterogeneous in ways that resist clean causal explanation. This chapter reframes that tension by treating diagnostic categories not as originating causes, but as artifacts of post-collapse stabilization.

In this view, diagnoses describe what stabilization looks like from the outside, not how it forms from the inside.

### Why Diagnostic Categories Cluster

Clinical categories cluster where stabilization strategies cluster. When collapse forces reorganization under constraint, the number of viable regulatory solutions is limited. Systems converge on similar configurations not because they share an essence, but because the space of workable solutions is narrow.

Diagnostic labels emerge where these configurations are most visible. They capture recurring surface regularities produced by constrained reorganization, not discrete disease entities with independent etiologies.

Outcomes Mistaken for Origins

Many diagnostic formulations implicitly reverse causality. Stabilized configurations are treated as primary structures rather than as responses to collapse. The label becomes explanatory, even though it names the result of a process rather than its cause.

This inversion explains why diagnostic categories:
-	overlap extensively,
-	shift over time,
-	and fail to predict transition.

They are accurate descriptions of stabilized behavior, but incomplete explanations of how that behavior came to be.

### Why Categories Persist Despite Theoretical Disagreement

Diagnostic systems persist not because they are mechanistically complete, but because they are observationally useful. They allow clinicians to recognize patterns, anticipate certain behaviors, and communicate efficiently.

Their persistence reflects pragmatic value, not causal sufficiency.

Within the present framework, diagnostic categories are retained as descriptive shorthand while their explanatory role is reassigned.

### Mapping Without Replacement

This framework does not replace diagnostic systems. It repositions them.

Narrative, avoidant, power-based, psychotic, and non-convergent configurations map imperfectly onto existing categories. The mapping is many-to-many rather than one-to-one. This explains both why diagnoses often fit partially and why comorbidity is the rule rather than the exception.

The framework accounts for these phenomena without multiplying disorders.

### Why Some Diagnoses Appear More Stable

Certain diagnostic categories appear more stable because the underlying configurations are more durable under common environmental conditions. Power-based and rigid narrative stabilizations, for example, often persist because they are cost-efficient and environmentally supported.

This durability is frequently misread as trait permanence. In reality, it reflects comparative advantage under constraint.

### Implications for Change

When diagnoses are treated as identities, change appears unlikely. When they are treated as stabilization artifacts, change becomes conditional rather than categorical.

Change requires alteration of constraint, not correction of essence. This reframing clarifies why insight alone rarely destabilizes entrenched configurations, and why environmental shifts can produce rapid reorganization.

### Ethical Clarification

Reframing diagnostic categories as stabilization artifacts does not diminish suffering, responsibility, or impact. It removes moralization without removing accountability. Harm remains harm, regardless of how it is explained.

The framework explains structure; ethical judgment remains external to it.

### From Diagnosis to Structure

This chapter does not argue against diagnosis. It argues for structural literacy: the ability to recognize when a diagnostic label is describing a stabilized outcome rather than explaining a causal process.

With this distinction in place, diagnostic language can be used more carefully and less reified within both clinical and observational contexts.

### Transition to Limits

Understanding diagnoses as artifacts clarifies what the framework can illuminate—and what it cannot. The following chapter specifies those boundaries explicitly, ensuring that explanatory power does not drift into overclaim.
