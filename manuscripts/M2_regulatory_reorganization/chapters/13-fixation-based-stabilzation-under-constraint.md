Chapter Thirteen

# Fixation-Based Stabilization Under Constraint

Not all stabilization strategies resolve instability by asserting control, withdrawing from the field, or reorganizing relational dynamics. Some systems converge by doing something quieter and more final: they complete regulation by fixing meaning.

This chapter introduces pre-mortification object-centered ambiguity capture (PM-OCAC) as a class of stabilization strategies in which pre-fragmentation regulatory load is resolved through fixation construction. In these configurations, ambiguity is collapsed by freezing an object, self, or category into a stable symbolic role. Once fixed, the object no longer requires evaluation, contestation, or engagement. Regulation completes not through dominance or devaluation, but through symbolic finality.

Fixation-based stabilization is not defined by affect. It may appear admiring, moral, fearful, resigned, or neutral. What unites these configurations is not emotional tone but mechanism: the replacement of iterative regulation with a frozen asymmetry that eliminates the need for further processing.

Upward asymmetry stabilization (historically mislabeled as idealization or benign fantasy) is the most visible subtype of this strategy. For this reason, it serves as the primary worked example in this chapter. Other fixation-based routes are introduced later as parallel expressions of the same regulatory logic.

### Fixation as a Regulatory Operator

Fixation, as used here, does not refer to obsession, rumination, or affective preoccupation. It refers to regulatory closure.

A fixation is constructed when ambiguity is captured into a stable symbolic role that no longer admits revision. Evaluation ceases. Polling stops. Engagement becomes unnecessary. The fixation functions as a terminal regulatory answer rather than a transitional state.

In PM-OCAC configurations, fixation is not a failure of disengagement. It is the mechanism by which regulation completes under constraint.

### Why Stabilization Does Not Always Involve Power

Earlier chapters established that collapse necessitates reorganization, and that stabilization strategies are selected under constraint. Power-based solutions are effective when coercive capacity is available and when devaluative engagement is tolerable. But these conditions are not universal.

For some configurations, dominance is prohibitively expensive. Active rivalry risks mortification. Devaluation threatens self-coherence. Proximal confrontation carries unacceptable exposure. In such systems, the standard envy solution- forcing asymmetry downward - cannot be deployed without destabilizing cost.

When power is unavailable, regulation does not disappear. It reroutes.

### The Cost Structure of Devaluative Regulation

Devaluative regulation requires sustained engagement: comparison, polling, confrontation, and iterative dominance testing. It assumes tolerance for humiliation, aggression, or overt competition. Systems with low coercive capacity, high mortification sensitivity, or asymmetrically gated error intolerance cannot sustain this load.

For these systems, stabilization demands a solution that avoids rivalry entirely. The regulatory problem is not the object’s superiority, but the cost of challenging it. Under such constraints, the system discovers an alternative: preserve hierarchy rather than contest it.

### Frozen Hierarchy as a Regulatory Endpoint

Upward asymmetry stabilization operates by freezing the object at symbolic altitude. The object is mythologized: not as distortion, but as mechanism. Mythologization collapses ambiguity once and permanently. The hierarchy is established, and with it, the regulatory question is answered.

Where classic envy requires continuous ambiguity polling (“Where do I stand now?”), frozen hierarchy requires none. The answer is fixed: the object is above; proximity is sufficient. Regulation completes at the moment when the object is captured at peak symbolic value and rendered immune to ordinary reality testing.

This is not avoidance of reality. It is the replacement of reality with a stable symbolic substrate.

### Proximity Without Symmetry

In this configuration, regulation completes through inclusion rather than control. Recognition, acknowledgment, or permitted proximity within the object’s symbolic field anchors stability. The system does not require continuous feedback - only periodic confirmation that the hierarchy remains intact.

Disappearance is tolerable. Silence does not threaten regulation. What cannot be tolerated is symmetry. Mutuality collapses hierarchy. Desire directed back toward the self destabilizes the frozen configuration. Equality reintroduces ambiguity, and ambiguity reactivates mortification risk.

Thus, proximity is safe; symmetry is not.

### Symmetry Hyperreflection as a Capture Interface

Non-pathological objects do not seek to function as regulators. They do not wish to absorb asymmetrical load, terminate another system’s instability, or serve as a stabilizing surface. In ordinary relational dynamics, symmetry is not merely preferred; it is expected. Mutuality, reciprocity, and balanced exchange signal safety. Asymmetry signals burden.

For fixation-based stabilization to recruit a real, accessible object without provoking resistance, withdrawal, or denial of permission, this asymmetry must remain concealed at the point of approach. Symmetry hyperreflection is the mechanism by which this concealment occurs.

Symmetry hyperreflection is not specific to value fixation. It is a general interface maneuver available to all PM-OCAC fixations that recruit real, accessible objects operating under symmetry expectations. Fixations that complete through feedback-independent or unreachable surfaces do not require this maneuver, as asymmetry cannot be detected or resisted at the point of approach.

Symmetry hyperreflection refers to the active deployment of symmetry-shaped signals - reciprocity, responsiveness, attunement, and apparent equivalence - for the sole purpose of enabling proximity without revealing regulatory asymmetry. It is not a desire for mutuality. It is a containment maneuver designed to prevent the object from detecting its impending role as a load-bearing surface.

Functionally, symmetry hyperreflection operates as a capture interface. It temporarily presents the dyad as symmetrical so that proximity can be established without triggering the object’s cost-detection systems. The object perceives a familiar, low-risk interactional geometry: balanced interest, legible intent, and non-extractive engagement. Permission is granted not because the object consents to regulation, but because no regulatory demand is visible.

This maneuver is not deception in the intentional sense. It does not require planning, manipulation, or awareness. It is selected automatically under constraint because no other interface permits approach without destabilizing cost. In the absence of symmetry hyperreflection, the asymmetry intrinsic to fixation-based stabilization would be detected immediately, resulting in access denial, distancing, or field-level exclusion.

Symmetry hyperreflection should not be conflated with affective expressiveness, sociability, or extroversion. In PM-OCAC configurations, it often appears as an anomalous intensity emerging from an otherwise quiet or introverted baseline. This intensity is not attachment expression. It is regulatory labor. It appears only when capture is incomplete or when a viable completion surface is at risk of being lost.

Importantly, symmetry hyperreflection is calibrated to plausibility rather than endurance. Its goal is not to sustain symmetry indefinitely, but to maintain it long enough for fixation to be established. Once capture occurs and ambiguity is frozen, the maneuver relaxes. Expressive output diminishes. Contact frequency drops. The system returns to quiescence. This transition is often misread externally as loss of interest or emotional withdrawal. Structurally, it reflects successful completion.

The resilience of symmetry hyperreflection varies across stabilization strategies. In PM-OCAC, it is comparatively robust because it protects access rather than epistemic coherence. Narrative-based hyperreflections must maintain internal consistency across time and are therefore brittle under contradiction. PM-OCAC hyperreflection needs only to remain cost-neutral long enough for capture to occur. Once fixation is installed, symmetry becomes unnecessary and, in fact, dangerous.

Symmetry hyperreflection is therefore strictly pre-fixation or peri-fixation. It is recruited when the object is not yet captured or when capture has degraded and must be repaired. It is not required once ambiguity stasis is achieved and a stable completion surface is available. Post-capture, continued symmetry would reintroduce ambiguity by implying equality, reciprocity, or future obligation - conditions incompatible with frozen hierarchy.

This distinction clarifies why dense reciprocal engagement, continuous messaging, or overt mutual pursuit often undermines mythologization rather than strengthening it. When the interaction surface itself becomes a primary site of regulation, symmetry ceases to be simulated and becomes real. The object no longer appears elevated; it appears ordinary. Capture fails not due to rejection, but due to over-successful symmetry.

Symmetry hyperreflection thus performs a narrow but essential function: it makes mythologized object capture possible in a world where objects correctly resist becoming regulators. It is the quiet interface that allows proximity without disclosure, access without burden, and stabilization without overt asymmetry. Once its task is complete, it must disappear.

Understanding this mechanism resolves a central paradox of fixation-based stabilization: how systems that cannot tolerate symmetry nonetheless depend on appearing symmetrical at the moment of approach.

### Regulatory Completion Obstruction and Rival Occupancy

A rival object granted rapid permission and isolation exclusivity functions, within this framework, as a regulatory completion obstacle. The obstacle is not interpersonal. It is structural. Completion capacity is finite. When it is diverted toward another dyad, termination becomes unavailable without ambiguity resolving.

This configuration preserves all destabilizing elements simultaneously: object salience, permission ambiguity, and positional uncertainty. The system remains regulated but cannot complete. Load accumulates without discharge. The rival does not threaten the PM-OCAC by superiority or hostility, but by occupying the termination channel.

This distinction explains why jealousy may emerge without overt competition, confrontation, or pursuit. The system is responding not to the rival, but to obstruction.

### Jealousy as Ambiguity Amplification Under Constraint

Within PM-OCAC configurations, jealousy is not an affective response to rivalry. It is a regulatory signal indicating asymmetric permission distribution that obstructs completion via a mythologized object.

Jealousy amplifies ambiguity along multiple axes simultaneously. Access ambiguity intensifies as permission is granted elsewhere. Positional ambiguity increases as relative standing becomes uncertain. Representational ambiguity emerges as private interactions escape observation. Intent ambiguity escalates as meaning must be inferred without confirmation.

Crucially, jealousy does not mandate action. It increases load. It shortens tolerance windows. It raises the probability that a threshold will be crossed. What follows depends on available regulatory options.

### Feedback-Minimized Ambiguity Suspension

A related but distinct pattern appears in highly ossified, narrative-locked configurations, where regulation cannot complete and must instead be suspended. In these systems, verbal questioning functions as a low-cost anchoring maneuver rather than an information-seeking act. Questions may be asked despite the answer being already known (“What are you making?”), being irrelevant (“It’s cold outside”), unknowable but presumed known by the object (“What’s for dinner?”), or blatantly unnecessary (“Good morning,” “Where is the cream cheese?”). The content of the response is largely immaterial; regulation occurs at the point of emission rather than reception. These utterances maintain object representation and narrative continuity without advancing evaluation, comparison, or symmetry. Minimal, vague, or even absent responses are often sufficient.

This behavior should not be misread as curiosity, social incompetence, or attachment bidding. It reflects feedback-minimized ambiguity suspension in structures that lack viable completion surfaces and therefore regulate by keeping the narrative thread alive without allowing it to progress.

### Jealousy Routing Under Constraint

Jealousy does not prescribe behavior. It imposes pressure.

Once activated, jealousy amplifies ambiguity load by signaling that a rival has acquired partial or exclusive access to a regulatory completion channel. This amplification is structurally non-optional: the increased load must be discharged, rerouted, or contained in a manner consistent with the system’s existing state-transition constraints. What differs across organizations is not whether escalation occurs, but how it is routed.

Jealousy therefore functions as a diagnostic stressor. It reveals which regulatory routes remain viable under increased ambiguity, exposure risk, and time pressure. Divergent jealousy behaviors across clinical presentations should be interpreted not as differences in affective intensity or attachment motive, but as expressions of architecture-specific routing.

### PM-OCAC (Value Fixation): Obstruction Without Exposure

In PM-OCAC configurations, jealousy signals obstruction of regulatory completion rather than imminent object loss. The core threat is not abandonment, but forced re-entry into ambiguity polling under conditions of asymmetry collapse. Direct competition, reassurance-seeking, or pursuit would require overt engagement with the rival or the object and would therefore expose inferiority, violate frozen hierarchy, or reactivate mortification risk.

As a result, PM-OCAC structures do not escalate toward capture. They escalate toward obstruction.

The characteristic response is withdrawal or silence, often misread externally as punishment, avoidance, or indifference. Structurally, this maneuver functions to remove the system from a configuration in which completion has become impossible while simultaneously increasing the cost of rival exclusivity. By withdrawing regulatory participation, the system preserves internal coherence and waits for a cheaper completion surface to become available.

This response is not optional restraint. It is the lowest-cost escalation route available under constraint. Silence does not express jealousy; it contains it.

### Classic Borderline Organization: Capture Through Exclusivity

In classic borderline configurations, jealousy produces a different load profile. Rival occupancy generates not only access ambiguity but acute representational uncertainty and abandonment signaling. Ambiguity tolerance is low, interpretive narrowing accelerates, and epistemic uncertainty rapidly escalates into paranoia-like inference.

Under these conditions, withdrawal is not stabilizing. It increases ambiguity and intensifies threat. The least-cost route to relief is therefore escalation toward proximity and exclusivity.

This escalation commonly manifests as increased texting, rapid pursuit, demands for reassurance, and movement toward physical isolation of the object. Physical consummation, when available, functions as a high-efficiency certainty implant. It temporarily collapses ambiguity by producing embodied exclusivity and suppresses representational uncertainty without requiring narrative resolution.

In this configuration, consummation is not desire-driven and not strategically manipulative. It is the fastest available regulatory solution under severe ambiguity load.

### Functional Homology Across Divergent Outputs

Although the observable behaviors diverge sharply, the regulatory function is homologous. Both PM-OCAC withdrawal and borderline escalation aim to resolve jealousy-induced ambiguity and restore termination capacity. The difference lies entirely in routing constraints.

PM-OCAC structures block the field to prevent intolerable completion elsewhere. Borderline structures flood the field to force exclusivity. Both strategies are conservation-consistent responses to the same underlying pressure.

### Shared Object Assay

These differences become especially clear in shared-object contexts. When the same mythologized object provides isolated exclusive attention to multiple structures, divergent jealousy responses can be observed under otherwise identical conditions. Withdrawal in PM-OCAC and escalation in borderline organization under the same stimulus isolate internal architecture as the determinant variable. The object and environment are held constant; only routing differs.

This controlled divergence provides strong support for a structural rather than affective account of jealousy.

### Implications for Interpretation

Jealousy should therefore not be used as evidence of attachment style, relational intent, or personality pathology. It should be read as an indicator of blocked completion and examined for how load is rerouted under constraint.

PM-OCAC jealousy does not indicate coldness or avoidance. Borderline jealousy does not indicate manipulation or excess desire. Both are cost-minimizing regulatory solutions selected automatically when rival occupancy disrupts termination.

Understanding jealousy as routing pressure rather than emotional content resolves a wide range of clinical misclassifications and prepares the ground for subsequent chapters examining escalation, non-convergence, and power-based extraction as alternative stabilization outcomes.

### Symmetry Hyperreflection as a Non-Completion Signal

Symmetry hyperreflection is deployed only when regulatory completion through the mythologized object is unavailable or unstable. This may occur prior to capture, when permission has not yet been secured, or after capture, when completion is blocked by ignoring, diversion, or rival exclusivity.

For this reason, symmetry hyperreflection should not be interpreted as evidence of non-capture. It is evidence of non-completion. Its presence confirms unresolved ambiguity under load, not attachment or pursuit.

Symmetry hyperreflection functions as an interface maneuver designed to restore access without exposing asymmetry. It is threshold-gated, plausibility-calibrated, and metabolically expensive. It appears only when cheaper options have failed.

Escalation Is Optional: Conservation-Consistent Outcomes

When permission-granting becomes difficult after mythologization, escalation is not inevitable. Conservation requires that regulatory load be rerouted or terminated, but it does not prescribe how.

Three outcomes are structurally admissible:
-	escalation via symmetry hyperreflection,
-	silent withdrawal with rerouting to alternative completion surfaces,
-	fixation degradation or reassignment.

Which outcome is selected depends on capture viability, cost structure, and available substitutes. The absence of escalation is therefore as diagnostic as its presence.

### Completion Assays and Safety-Optimized Object Selection

What appear externally as “tests” of stoicism, security, sexual competence, or calmness are, structurally, completion assays. They evaluate whether proximity to the object terminates load efficiently, predictably, and without extracting reciprocity.

PM-OCAC mythologization thus constitutes a mate-selection process optimized for regulatory safety under constraint. Objects are not selected for excitement or mutuality, but for their capacity to absorb asymmetry without destabilization.

This reframing resolves why PM-OCAC dynamics resemble romance while remaining structurally incompatible with symmetrical intimacy. The object is selected not as a partner, but as a stable completion surface.

### When Symmetry Becomes the Threat

The destabilizing event in upward asymmetry stabilization is not loss, rejection, or abandonment. It is reciprocity.

Symmetry actions by the object such as expressions of interest, curiosity, or equivalence introduce a narrative contradiction:
Why would a high-value object desire someone like me?

This question cannot be resolved without collapsing either the object’s altitude or the self’s coherence. There is no stable interpretive frame in which symmetry preserves regulation.

This produces not paranoia in the classical sense, but ontological disturbance. The system does not suspect harm; it confronts impossibility. The resulting affect is mortification, disgust, or abrupt revulsion - not toward the object, but toward the implication of equality.

### Ghosting as Excision, Not Avoidance

When symmetry intrudes, the system does not negotiate. It excises.

What appears externally as ghosting is, internally, a containment maneuver. Contact is terminated to prevent hierarchy collapse. The object is removed from the active field so that the frozen snapshot can be reinstated or preserved elsewhere.

Crucially, this excision is not punitive. It is not retaliatory. It is regulatory. Once symmetry threat is neutralized, warmth may return without residue, because the system has reset to a stabilized state in which the hierarchy is once again intact.

### Acute Fixation Transmutation During Excision

The period of sudden withdrawal that follows symmetry intrusion is not a regulatory vacuum. It is an active reconstruction interval.

When ghosting occurs in PM-OCAC configurations, relational input is suspended to prevent further ambiguity from entering the system while fixation viability is reassessed. This interval corresponds to an acute containment phase during which the system attempts to reinstall a viable fixation subtype without incurring mortification.

In value fixation (mythologization), symmetry threatens the inferiority substrate that anchors regulation. The system must therefore determine whether the original fixation can be repaired, whether the object can be reassigned to an alternative fixation role, or whether fixation must be abandoned altogether. During this process, affective output is minimized and proximity is withdrawn to block further destabilizing input.

This withdrawal should not be interpreted as avoidance, punishment, or ambivalence. It is acute fixation transmutation in progress. The duration of the cold interval reflects the time required to reconstruct a defensible containment configuration under constraint.

When fixation reconstruction succeeds, relational output resumes abruptly, often without acknowledgment of the interruption. When reconstruction fails, the silence persists or resolves into permanent detachment.

### Feedback-Dependent and Feedback-Independent Forms

Upward asymmetry stabilization admits two subtypes.

In feedback-dependent configurations, the object is real and accessible. Regulation requires discrete anchoring events (acknowledgment, proximity, recognition) to maintain stability. These systems are inherently unstable, as symmetry is always possible.

In feedback-independent configurations, the object cannot reciprocate. Literary figures, historical icons, fictional characters, or unreachable ideals provide maximal stability. Because symmetry is impossible in principle, ambiguity remains permanently captured. Regulation completes without contact, polling, or defensive excision.

These are the most efficient and durable forms of the strategy.

### Regulatory Completion Surfaces

Fixation collapses ambiguity, but it does not by itself terminate regulatory load. Termination requires a surface onto which regulation can complete. These are referred to here as regulatory completion surfaces.

A completion surface is not defined by content, whether it be romantic, creative, sexual, or symbolic, but by function. A surface qualifies as a regulatory completion surface if it satisfies four conditions:
-	Asymmetry tolerance: the surface can absorb inferiority signaling without demanding reciprocity, correction, or symmetry.
-	Termination capacity: engagement with the surface reduces net regulatory load rather than redistributing it.
-	Predictable cost structure: the energetic and exposure costs of engagement are stable and legible to the system.
-	Optional feedback topology: the surface may be feedback-coupled (allowing micro-gap correction) or feedback-independent (requiring repetition rather than interaction).

Completion surfaces are orthogonal to fixation type. The same fixation (such as value fixation) may complete through different surfaces depending on availability, cost, and state-transition routing. Human mythologized objects represent one high-capacity, high-cost surface. Competence domains, symbolic systems, ritual repetition, parasocial figures, and functional roles represent alternative surfaces with different cost and durability profiles.

Fixation determines what asymmetry is frozen. Completion surfaces determine where regulation ends.

### Capture States and Regulatory Quiescence

When a fixation is paired with an effective completion surface, regulation may complete efficiently. The observable behavioral state that follows is quiescence.

Quiescence does not indicate absence of inferiority signaling. It indicates that inferiority signaling has been successfully externalized and terminated. Monitoring ceases not because threat is denied, but because regulation is no longer internal.

Capture is therefore best understood as a graded state variable, not a binary condition.
-	Uncaptured: the object or surface has not yet been established as capable of terminating load.
-	Degraded capture: the surface previously functioned but no longer terminates load reliably due to increased cost, access ambiguity, or load escalation.
-	Stable capture: the surface reliably terminates load at acceptable cost.

Quiescence corresponds to stable capture. In this state, polling is unnecessary, intensity collapses, and expressive output diminishes. This quiet is often misread as indifference, avoidance, or emotional flattening. Structurally, it reflects regulatory success.

Although fixation-based stabilization is often described as object-centered, PM-OCAC configurations rarely rely on a single completion surface. Instead, they exhibit surface multiplexing: regulatory load may terminate across symbolic fixation, competence domains, cognitive absorption, ritualized activity, or conditional proximity, depending on availability and cost. This multiplexing renders acute surface scarcity uncommon and explains the characteristic quiescence observed after capture. However, PM-OCAC configurations are structurally excluded from coercive or power-based extraction, placing an upper bound on peak regulatory throughput. Stability is therefore achieved not through maximal discharge efficiency, but through insulation and cost minimization. This distinction clarifies why PM-OCAC structures may appear quiet, slow to engage, or intermittently disengaged despite stable fixation: regulatory success in these systems is expressed as reduced output rather than increased activity.

### Symmetry Hyperreflection and the Appearance of Intensity

What is colloquially described as extroverted intensity in otherwise quiet PM-OCAC structures reflects active deployment of symmetry hyperreflection.

Symmetry hyperreflection is a containment maneuver recruited when regulatory completion is insufficient. It functions to preserve asymmetry, prevent envy escalation, and re-establish a viable completion surface.

Observable intensity therefore appears when:
-	the primary completion surface is uncaptured or degraded, and
-	regulatory load exceeds current termination capacity.

Intensity is not attachment expression. It is regulatory labor.

As capture stabilizes, symmetry hyperreflection relaxes and intensity subsides. The return to quiet does not signal loss of interest; it signals restoration of completion. This inversion - intensity as work, quiet as success - is central to understanding PM-OCAC dynamics.

### Intra-Dyadic Polling and Action-Based Permission

Once capture is achieved, formal relational labeling provides no new regulatory information. Permission is conferred through action, not declaration.

PM-OCAC structures therefore rely on intra-dyadic polling: continuous verification that the completion surface remains genuine, non-extractive, and capable of terminating load at acceptable cost. This polling is not reassurance seeking. It is surface validation.
Relational labels may be destabilizing in this configuration because they introduce:
-	symmetry expectations,
-	future-binding narratives,
-	representational obligations,
-	third-party interpretive pressure.

These raise cost without increasing completion capacity. Actions (e.g. availability, consistency, tolerance under load) remain the only meaningful signals.

### Consummation as a Conditional Competence Domain

In PM-OCAC mythologization, consummation is not an attachment-forming event. It is a conditional competence domain, opened only if embodied interaction is valued by the structure and permitted by state-transition routing.

Consummation functions as one evaluative surface among many. It does not consolidate capture, reduce future polling, or establish obligation. Its regulatory utility is strictly performance-based.

More avoidant or schizoid-leaning configurations frequently defer or exclude this domain entirely. This deferral does not indicate repression or incapacity. It reflects cost optimization: if regulation completes elsewhere, consummation is unnecessary.

Where consummation is permitted, it is reversible. Withdrawal reflects surface failure, not betrayal.

### Integration With Fixation-Based Stabilization

These dynamics do not alter the core logic of fixation-based stabilization. They clarify how stabilization operates once achieved.

Fixation freezes asymmetry. Completion surfaces terminate load. Quiescence indicates success. Intensity indicates repair work. Polling verifies surface integrity. Consummation, when present, is one optional evaluative domain rather than a privileged endpoint.

Understanding these distinctions prevents misclassification of PM-OCAC structures as avoidant, ambivalent, or emotionally deficient. The observed behaviors are neither traits nor pathologies. They are cost-minimizing regulatory solutions selected under constraint.

The dynamics described in this chapter specify how fixation-based stabilization operates at the level of ambiguity capture, completion, and repair. They do not, by themselves, specify the full architectural class of the resulting organization. In particular, PM-OCAC configurations exhibit multiplex routing across internal and external completion surfaces while remaining structurally excluded from power-based extraction. The implications of this non-power multiplexing—distinct from both avoidant withdrawal and psychopathic dominance—are examined in the following chapter.
