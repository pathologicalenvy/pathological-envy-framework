Chapter Fourteen

# Non-Power Multiplex Stabilization Under Constraint

The preceding chapter established fixation-based stabilization as a viable regulatory solution under constraint and formalized pre-mortification object-centered ambiguity capture (PM-OCAC) as a mechanism by which regulation can complete without dominance, avoidance, or narrative reconstruction. What remained unresolved was the architectural status of this solution. Fixation explains how ambiguity is collapsed; it does not, by itself, specify what kind of system emerges once collapse stabilizes.

This chapter addresses that gap. It demonstrates that PM-OCAC - specifically in its value-fixation, upward-asymmetry form (PM-OCAC (VF)) - constitutes a distinct post-collapse configuration: a non-power-locked multiplex stabilization architecture. This architecture is capable of both internal and external regulatory completion while remaining structurally excluded from dominance-based extraction, coercion, or narrative control. Its quietness is not an absence of structure. It is evidence of a different kind of organization.

Fixation, as defined in the previous chapter, captures ambiguity into a stable symbolic role. When PM-OCAC (VF), the value-fixation (mythologization) subtype, is deployed, completion surfaces then terminate regulatory load. Quiescence marks success. Repair operations preserve viability under threat. At the architectural level, these elements do not function as a linear sequence. They coexist as a multiplex routing system in which regulatory load can be absorbed, terminated, or rerouted across multiple internal and external surfaces without invoking rivalry, coercion, or confrontation.

This is the defining feature of PM-OCAC (VF) multiplexing: regulation is solved without winning, without extracting from others, and without forcing asymmetry downward. The system does not seek dominance. It seeks termination.

### Developmental Bias Toward Upward Asymmetry

Environmental conditions do not create this architecture; they reveal its viability.

Developmental contexts characterized by saturated hierarchies where attention, prestige, protection, or rescue are already occupied select against competitive solutions. In such environments, contesting hierarchy is metabolically expensive and exposure-heavy. Rivalry destabilizes the self without reorganizing the field.

Under these constraints, upward asymmetry becomes the safest regulatory option. Proximity to value stabilizes more reliably than competition for it. Hierarchy is treated as fixed rather than negotiable. Regulation is achieved not by altering rank, but by anchoring beneath it.

This bias reflects cost optimization under irreversible constraint, not passivity or weakness.

### Pathology by Relational Necessity

Upward asymmetry stabilization is pathological not because it is hostile, manipulative, or exploitative, but because it forecloses mutuality.

A symmetric relationship would require dismantling the very hierarchy that terminates regulation. Equality reintroduces ambiguity. Ambiguity reactivates mortification risk. For stability to hold, the object must remain elevated, unreal, or unreachable.

This is why PM-OCAC structures may appear warm, relational, or attentive while remaining structurally incompatible with intimacy. The pathology lies not in intent, but in necessity. The object must remain a completion surface - not a partner.

### The Mirror of the Classic Envy Cycle

PM-OCAC represents a true inversion of the classic envy solution.

Where classic envy resolves threat by degrading the object, PM-OCAC resolves threat by elevating it.

Where classic envy requires continuous ambiguity polling, PM-OCAC freezes ambiguity into fixation.

Where classic envy mobilizes paranoia under loss, PM-OCAC mobilizes mortification under symmetry.

Both are stable solutions under constraint. They differ only in the direction asymmetry is forced. This symmetry of inversion confirms that PM-OCAC is not a deviation from the envy cycle, but one of its canonical endpoints.

### Dynamic Stability and Local Containment

Fixation-based stabilization is durable, but not invulnerable. When events reintroduce ambiguity that cannot be metabolized within the existing symbolic role, the system does not immediately reorganize. It attempts local containment.

Three outcomes are structurally admissible:
-	Fixation reinstatement: ambiguity intrusion is judged reparable. Contact is excised, symbolic reinforcement increases, and the original fixation is restored.
-	Fixation reassignment: repair is infeasible but detachment unnecessary. The same object is retained under a downgraded or altered symbolic role.
-	Fixation annihilation: contradiction is too coherent or exposed to permit reassignment. The object is expelled entirely to prevent fragmentation.

These outcomes are governed by repair feasibility and mortification magnitude—not affective intensity.

### Reinforced Reconstitution and Warmth Amplification

In some cases, fixation reinstatement returns with increased warmth, expressiveness, or proximity. This amplification is not relational growth. It is reinforced containment.

Following near-failure, the system reinstalls the same fixation subtype with heightened symbolic reinforcement to prevent recurrence. Because repair occurs internally, no explanation or negotiation is required. Externally, this is often misread as renewed attachment. Structurally, it signals successful ambiguity capture without integration of destabilizing information.

### Parallel Fixation Subtypes Within One Architecture

Mythologization is only one expression of PM-OCAC multiplexing.

The same architectural logic supports multiple fixation targets, including culpability fixation, threat fixation, defect fixation, access fixation, and inferiority fixation. These subtypes differ in target and asymmetry direction, not in mechanism.

Each represents a distinct method of freezing ambiguity to terminate regulatory load under constraint.

### Non-Power Multiplexing Versus Power-Based Multiplexing

PM-OCAC multiplexing must be sharply distinguished from psychopathic or dominance-based multiplex architectures.

Power-based systems extract regulation through coercion, fear, or control. PM-OCAC terminates regulation by minimizing engagement cost and foreclosing rivalry. It preserves sovereignty without exploiting the object.

This distinction explains why PM-OCAC structures may appear ethical, calm, or benign while remaining rigid and non-reciprocal. They are multiplexed, but non-extractive. Stabilized, but not flexible.

### Quiet Brittleness and Minimal Reversibility

Upward asymmetry stabilization completes regulation efficiently and quietly. Its brittleness lies not in instability, but in irreversibility.

Once regulation depends on preserved asymmetry, symmetry becomes structurally impossible. What appears calm conceals an absolute prohibition against equality.

This configuration is not marginal. It is a primary stabilization route available to specific state-transition geometries under constraint. Its effects are subtle. Its reversibility is minimal. Its consequences are total.
