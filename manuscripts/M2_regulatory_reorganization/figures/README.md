# Figures — M2

- Figure 1. Collapse disrupts regulatory coherence and forces reorganization. What follows is not adjustment within an existing structure, but the construction of a new one.
- Figure 2. Stabilization restores functional coherence without implying repair, closure, or optimality.
- Figure 3. Constraint limits which outcomes are viable after collapse without determining which path will be taken. Ambiguity is represented as loss of connectivity or access between regions, not as a state or transition.
- Figure 4. Ambiguity Resolution Windows and the Solvability Funnel. As predictive solvability declines following object withdrawal, regulation externalizes through progressively less reversible resolution mechanisms. Systems move through this space at different speeds and along different routes depending on constraint and state-transition bias; escalation reflects loss of solvability rather than increasing intent or affect. Ambiguity is represented as loss of connectivity or access between regions, not as a state or transition.
- Figure 5. State-transition directors bias reorganization toward lower-cost stabilizations without fixing endpoints.
- Figure 6. Ambiguity without exit describes a regulatory condition in which no available route produces legitimate resolution. Action is constrained, withdrawal fails to reduce load, and narrative collapse is either premature or too costly to sustain. The system remains active and regulated, but progress is suspended. This state is defined not by lack of information, but by the absence of an executable solution.
