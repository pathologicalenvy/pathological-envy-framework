Figure 9b. Interpersonal regulatory failure and addiction routing.
When regulation cannot be completed within interpersonal space—due to blocked feedback, intolerable exposure, unresolved asymmetry, or escalating shame—continued relational engagement increases cost without restoring coherence. Once this cost exceeds the system’s capacity for internal or reciprocal correction, regulation is not abandoned but rerouted. Addiction emerges here as a non-interpersonal regulatory surface that provides predictable, non-reciprocal, and immediate loop closure through negative reinforcement. This rerouting stabilizes the system transiently while leaving the original interpersonal failure unresolved, progressively narrowing alternative regulatory pathways and increasing persistence under constraint. Addiction in this account is not a preference, trait, or moral outcome, but a structurally cheaper solution to an otherwise unsolvable regulatory demand.
