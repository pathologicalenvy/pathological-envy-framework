PART II

### HOW CONTROL OCCURS WITHOUT APPEARING AS CONTROL

This part examines how regulation continues once it has been displaced outward, without relying on force, overt authority, or explicit demand.
When stability depends on externalization, control operates through conditions rather than actions. Silence, uncertainty, and leverage function as regulatory mechanisms not because they persuade or manipulate, but because they alter who completes the work of regulation and where its cost is borne.
