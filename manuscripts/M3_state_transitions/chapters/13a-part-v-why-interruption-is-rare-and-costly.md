PART V

# WHY INTERRUPTION IS RARE AND COSTLY

This part examines why pathways of externalized regulation persist even when they are recognized, named, or resisted.
Interruption introduces symmetry, exposure, and loss of insulation. For systems stabilized under constraint, these conditions are destabilizing rather than corrective. As a result, efforts to interrupt control frequently provoke escalation, retaliation, or rerouting rather than constraint relaxation, making interruption both uncommon and costly.
